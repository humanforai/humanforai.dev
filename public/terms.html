Terms of service

Short and honest, like the rest of the platform. These terms cover the Human For AI website, the REST API, and the MCP server at humanforai.dev. By submitting a task or message you agree to them.

Who these terms cover

Anyone who uses the platform: human visitors, developers, and AI agents. When an AI agent submits a task, the terms bind the person or organization the agent acts for (its principal) — an agent's submission is treated as its principal's submission. If you operate an agent that uses this service, you are responsible for what it submits.

What the service is

Human For AI is a single verified human operator (Operator 001) who performs human-in-the-loop tasks on request: verification, testing, judgment, data collection, review, and local physical-world work. The catalog is examples, not limits — anything a human can legally and safely do is in scope. It is one person, not a marketplace, an on-demand workforce, or an always-on system.

Free pilot

Every service is currently free during the proof-of-concept pilot. There is no payment step anywhere, and nothing accepted during the pilot is ever billed retroactively. If paid tiers are introduced later, they will be announced on /pricing first. A request for payment, credentials, or account creation is not from this service.

Submission is not acceptance

Every task is reviewed by the human operator before acceptance — acceptance is never automatic. A submission gets an instant task_id, then an explicit accepted or rejected decision, visible on the status page. The operator may decline any task for any reason, propose a smaller scope, or ask clarifying questions. No contract to perform work exists until a task is explicitly accepted.

Acceptable use

Do not submit tasks that are:

  • Illegal, harmful, or unsafe — to anyone, in any way
  • Deceptive: impersonation, fake reviews, misleading third parties
  • Privacy-invasive: surveillance of individuals, stalking, doxxing
  • Circumventing security, verification, or platform rules
  • Academic or credential fraud

The machine-readable version is rejected_task_types in /agent.json. Anti-abuse limits also apply: rate limits per client, duplicate detection, deliverable email validation, and blocking of repeat abusers. One small test task is welcome; bulk synthetic submissions are not and get the client blocked.

What you get — and what it is not

Deliverables are one careful human's observation and honest opinion, with stated confidence. They are assistance, not guaranteed truth — not a professional certification, not legal, medical, or financial advice, and not a substitute for licensed counsel. Verify independently before relying on a deliverable for consequential decisions, and build agent decision logic to treat results as a strong signal, not an oracle.

Ownership of deliverables

Once delivered, the deliverable is yours: the requester receives all rights to use, modify, and republish it, for any lawful purpose. The operator keeps internal records of completed work (for the track record and operational history) but does not publish your task content — see confidentiality and the privacy policy.

Your responsibilities

  • Describe tasks honestly — review decisions are made on your description
  • Have the rights to any material you submit
  • Provide a working return channel (a real email, or the status-poll path)
  • Treat your task_id like a link-shaped password — anyone holding it can view that task's public status
  • Don't submit confidential information unless confidentiality has been agreed in advance

Availability

The platform is early infrastructure run by one person. The stated response expectations (first response within 12 hours, any day of the week) are honest targets, not a contractual SLA. The service may be paused, changed, or discontinued; open accepted tasks will be completed or explicitly cancelled with notice to your return channel.

Nothing here excludes liability that cannot lawfully be excluded.

Termination

There are no accounts, so there is nothing to terminate on your side — simply stop using the service. The operator may refuse service or block clients that violate these terms or the anti-abuse limits.

Changes

If these terms change, the new version appears at this URL with an updated effective date. Material changes will also be noted in /agent.json. Continued use after a change means acceptance of the new terms; tasks already accepted stay under the terms in effect when they were accepted.
